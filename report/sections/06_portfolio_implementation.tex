The U100 portfolio achieved positive net returns but with extreme concentration in low-liquidity stocks. The U50 portfolio---the pre-registered primary execution universe---failed to produce positive absolute returns, despite generating positive benchmark-relative excess.

\subsection{The Excess-Return Trap}
Table~\ref{tab:portfolio} illustrates the problem: U50 RESID generates +4.9\% annual excess over a declining benchmark of -5.9, but an investor allocating capital to the strategy would still lose money in absolute terms.

\subsection{Cost Analysis}
Transaction costs accounted for approximately 4.2 percentage points of annual return drag. The primary cost drivers were stamp tax (5bp on all sells, roughly 3.2\% p.a.) and commission plus slippage (6bp per round trip, approximately 1.0\% p.a.).
